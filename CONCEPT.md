# Research methodology

Generalized from the Dzaleka situation analysis (Sept 2026). This is the
template every file in `docs/cases/` should follow, so cases stay
comparable across sites.

## Purpose

Build the evidence base a sector-level Theory of Change should be built
from — **not the ToC itself**. A case file documents the situation as it
is, sourced and dated, across four pathways. It stops short of prescribing
an intervention; that comes later, after cross-case comparison surfaces
where the opportunity actually is.

## The four pathways

Applied consistently per case, though relative weight/relevance varies by
site:

1. **Legal / policy** — the structural rule-set the population lives under
   (encampment policy, freedom of movement, right to work, legal status
   pathways, treaty reservations). What is the binding constraint, and is
   there a live reform process?
2. **Funding sustainability** — is the humanitarian response resourced, at
   what trend, and how exposed is it to donor concentration or global
   funding shocks?
3. **Disaster / risk exposure** — hazards (fire, flood, disease, conflict
   spillover), referral/evacuation capacity, and the documented (or
   undocumented) institutional interface for emergency response.
4. **Protection & vulnerable groups** — child protection, GBV, trafficking,
   crime trends, and existing self-advocacy/civic structures.

## Required sections per case file

1. **Baseline profile** — table: established, location, design capacity,
   current population (with date), demographic composition, growth
   trajectory, host community context.
2. **Pathway A–D** — one section each, table-first, every figure sourced
   and dated.
3. **Cross-pathway causal skeleton** — a small ASCII/mermaid diagram
   showing how the pathways causally interact at this site (which pathway
   is the pivot node, if any).
4. **Evidence gaps** — flagged explicitly, not filled. This section is as
   important as the data itself: it tells the comparison pass where a case
   is under-researched versus genuinely low-signal.
5. **Draft ToC skeleton** — problem statement, candidate long-term outcome,
   candidate intermediate outcomes (one per pathway), key assumptions to
   stress-test. Marked as draft/not final.

## Sourcing discipline

- Every figure: original source, publication date, reporting period.
- Conflicting figures across sources are kept, not reconciled — note the
  likely reason (reporting lag, methodology, contested count).
- Single-sourced or non-mainstream claims are flagged explicitly as
  unverified.
- Use Tavily (web search + extract) for primary research; prefer UNHCR,
  IOM, WFP, ReliefWeb, national disaster-management bodies, and peer-
  reviewed/academic sources over aggregators where possible.

## When to move from breadth to narrowing

Not on a fixed case count. Narrow when a comparison across cases (in
`docs/comparison.md`) shows a lever that is simultaneously: high-leverage
(sits upstream of multiple bad outcomes, per the causal skeletons),
tractable (a concrete, fundable, actionable intervention exists), and
neglected (not already well-resourced/well-covered by existing actors)
relative to the other cases researched. Until then, keep gathering.
