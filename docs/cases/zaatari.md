# Zaatari Theory of Change — Situation Analysis

Broad-to-narrow situation research for a sector-level Theory of Change (ToC)
covering Zaatari Refugee Camp, Mafraq Governorate, Jordan (Syrian refugee
crisis). Compiled via Tavily web research and search, September 2026. **Not
the ToC itself yet** — this is the evidence base the ToC's problem tree,
pathways, and assumptions should be built from. Follows the same sourcing
discipline as `dzaleka.md`: every figure keeps its original source, date,
and reporting period; nothing is silently reconciled across conflicting
numbers.

A note on the numbers: Zaatari's population figures move faster than most
comparison cases right now, and in the opposite direction from Dzaleka —
**down**, sharply, since the fall of the Assad government in December 2024
triggered a wave of voluntary returns to Syria. Population reported at any
given date should be read against that trend line, not treated as a stable
baseline. A second note, flagged up front because it shapes every pathway
below: **Zaatari is the outlier in this comparison set for infrastructure
maturity** — mains-grid-connected solar power, a formalized market
(the "Champs-Élysées"), elected/appointed neighborhood committees, and a
paved, gridded urban layout are all present, a product of the camp's 2013
governance restructuring and over a decade of donor capital investment.
This case file tries to capture that nuance rather than flattening Zaatari
into a generic emergency-camp profile.

**Refreshed 2026-09-22** after a Tavily API-key fix; re-ran research across
all four pathways. Two of this file's flagged gaps are resolved this pass
(the 2026 Jordan financial requirement, and the undated WFP cash-cut
figure), and one key assumption is complicated: the return wave looks to
be decelerating in 2026 relative to 2025's peak, not continuing at pace.
Updates are flagged inline as "Update, Sept 2026."

- **Electricity:** 12.9 MW solar plant (KfW/Germany-funded, Jordan Ministry
  of Energy co-financing), operational Nov 2017 — at the time the largest
  solar plant in any UNHCR-run camp; connects 100% of shelters via a
  renovated medium/low-voltage grid; surplus power feeds back into Jordan's
  national grid (~15,000 tonnes/year CO2 reduction attributed to this).
  Refugee electricians trained and JICA-accredited maintain the system.
  [KfW Stories](https://www.kfw.de/stories/society/social-cohesion/solar-plant-zaatari);

- **Market economy:** an estimated 3,000 refugee-operated shops and
  businesses as of Dec 2015 along the camp's main commercial street,
  nicknamed "Champs-Élysées"; WFP shifted from in-kind food distribution to
  a voucher system redeemable at camp shops, later to an iris-scan biometric
  payment system (76,000 refugees using it at one point). [UNHCR/ReliefWeb
  factsheet, Dec 2015](https://reliefweb.int/report/jordan/zaatari-refugee-camp-factsheet-december-2015);
  [Refugees International](https://www.refugeesinternational.org/the-difference-a-market-makes-the-case-of-syrian-refugee-camps-in-jordan);

- **Governance/district structure:** camp restructured into 12
  districts/neighborhoods under the 2013 Zaatari Governance Plan, each with

  school enrollment, health, and similar issues. An academic study (2025)
  flags a caveat worth carrying forward: ~45% of civic committee seats are
  held by members of a small number of dominant clans, a documented
  elite-capture risk within an otherwise participatory structure. [UNHCR,
  "Jordan's Za'atari refugee camp takes off"](https://www.unhcr.org/news/stories/jordans-zaatari-refugee-camp-takes);

## 2. Pathway A — Legal / policy reform

**Root structural fact.** Jordan is **not a signatory to the 1951 Refugee
Convention or its 1967 Protocol**. There is no national asylum law. The
entire legal basis for UNHCR's presence and for refugee protection in
Jordan is a **1998 Memorandum of Understanding (MoU)** between the
Government of Jordan and UNHCR (partially amended 2014), which adopts a
Convention-like refugee definition and commits Jordan to non-refoulement,
but is not itself a binding international instrument and grants none of
the property, employment, or residency rights the 1951 Convention would.
[UNHCR Global Appeal 2015 update](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/legacy-pdf/5461e6070.pdf);
[Janmyr, "1951 Refugee Convention and Non-Signatory States" (2019)](https://academic.oup.com/ijrl/article/33/2/188/6448830);
[PMC, "Jordan and Syrian humanitarian refugees' dilemma"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9096676)

**Reading:** unlike Dzaleka, where the legal pathway is about *unlocking* a
rigid encampment policy, Zaatari's legal pathway is currently defined by a
**live, accelerating transition toward camp wind-down and return**, driven
by a regional political shock (Assad's fall) rather than a domestic
legal-reform process. The open question this pathway raises for a ToC is
not "how do we get people out of the camp" (Dzaleka's framing) but "what
happens to camp infrastructure, services, and the residual population who
are not returning, as the camp's population and funding both fall faster
than the physical/administrative footprint can be wound down."

## 3. Pathway B — Funding sustainability

**This is the same global structural funding shock documented in the
Dzaleka case file, compounded in Jordan's case by donor reprioritization
away from a crisis increasingly perceived (rightly or not) as "resolving
itself" via returns.** UN OCHA's 2026 aid appeal fell to $23 billion
overall — the lowest since 2017 — following the US drawdown of its aid
budget in 2025; the Syria regional response specifically saw 2026
requirements estimated ~40% lower than 2025, reflecting an explicit
"hyper-prioritization" exercise, not just lower need. [Global Humanitarian
Overview 2026, Syria Regional
Response](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/syria-regional-response)

**Documented downstream mechanism, same shape as Dzaleka's:** funding
shortfall → WFP retargeting/ration cuts → reduced household purchasing
power inside a camp whose market economy (Champs-Élysées) depends on
refugee cash spending → contraction of the very informal economy that made
Zaatari's "governance dividend" model work in the first place. This is a
Zaatari-specific twist worth flagging: because Zaatari's economy is more
monetized and market-based than a typical camp, funding cuts likely
transmit through the *local economy*, not just through household welfare —
but this transmission mechanism is not directly evidenced in the sources
reviewed and should be treated as a hypothesis for a follow-up pass, not a
documented finding.

- **UNDP** runs an institutional-capacity DRR project for Jordan

## 6. Cross-pathway causal skeleton

```
[Assad regime falls, Dec 2024] ──┬──→ [Mass voluntary returns] ──→ [Camp population halves in ~18 months: 76k→47k]
                                  │                                          │
                                  │                                          └──→ [Camp's fixed infrastructure (solar grid,
                                  │                                               market, district committees) now serves a
                                  │                                               shrinking, more-residual population — an
                                  │                                               under-documented sustainability question]
                                  │
                                  └──→ [Donor reprioritization: Syria seen as "resolving"] ──→ [3RP/JRP funding cut ~30-40%]
                                                                                                        │
                                                     ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                                     ▼
                                          [WFP ration/cash cuts] ──→ [Household purchasing power ↓] ──→ [Champs-Élysées
                                                                                                          market economy
                                                                                                          contracts]
                                                     │
                                                     └──→ [Economic pressure on remaining households] ──→ [Child marriage ↑
                                                                                                            (35% Jordan-wide;
                                                                                                            local surge cited)]

[No 1951 Convention accession; 1998 MoU is the entire legal basis] ──→ [Work-permit regime always conditional, not rights-based]
                                                     │
                                                     └──→ [2024 Jordan Compact subsidy rollback] ──→ [93% of working refugees
                                                                                                       lack valid permits] ──→
                                                                                                       [reinforces aid dependence
                                                                                                       just as aid is cut]

[Undocumented JCD ↔ UNHCR/SRAD evacuation interface] ← [structurally unaffected by the return wave — this gap persists
                                                          regardless of population trend, and shrinks in visibility/priority
                                                          as international attention shifts to returns]
```

**Reading:** Zaatari's pivot node is different from Dzaleka's. At Dzaleka,
funding collapse degrades a *fixed or growing* population's protection and
disaster-risk exposure. At Zaatari, funding collapse and population
collapse are **happening simultaneously and from the same root cause**
(the Assad regime's fall), which could read as a self-resolving situation —
but the sources reviewed suggest the opposite risk: a **residual
population** (the more vulnerable, more return-reluctant households) is
left behind as services, market activity, and international attention all
contract in step with the population, potentially *faster* than the
population itself declines. The disaster-risk pathway (C) is the one
pathway in this file that is structurally decoupled from the return
dynamic — the JCD↔UNHCR/SRAD evacuation-interface gap does not close just
because fewer people are exposed to it, and a shrinking camp draws less
monitoring attention to a gap that was never well-documented to begin
with.

  flagged as most critical in the Dzaleka case file.
- No camp-level, current flood/drainage capacity map found for this
  research pass (REACH's flood-risk and storm-water maps are dated Oct/Nov
  2015; no more recent equivalent located).
- ~~2026 Jordan Response Plan (JRP) total requirement figure not located~~

  $650.5M with a full sector breakdown (Pathway B, §3), though it is not
  fully reconciled against the JRP-labeled 2024/2025 figures already in
  this file — the JRP and 3RP figures may not be perfectly like-for-like.
- The OCHA FTS "3RP funding against Jordan" figure ($65.1M / $2,800.7M,
  2.3%) is ambiguous — it is unclear whether this represents Jordan-specific
  funding against the regional requirement or a data-attribution artifact;
  needs direct verification against UNHCR/OCHA Jordan-specific tracking.
- ~~WFP's one-third cash-assistance cut for Zaatari + Azraq ($32→$21/person/
  month) is undated~~ — **resolved, Sept 2026 refresh:** confirmed as a
  2023 event (Pathway B, §3); the current (2026) camp rate remains $21,
  unchanged since.
- Zaatari-specific child-marriage statistics (2-in-5 girls, 12% surge since
  2019, 321 underage marriages "this year") trace to a single source
  (Wilson Center); the better-corroborated figure is the Jordan-wide
  UNICEF 35% statistic, which is not camp-specific.
- No current (2025-2026) camp-level crime/security incident data found —
  the security/policing sources retrieved date mostly to 2013-2018; unclear
  whether security incidents have risen or fallen alongside the population
  decline.
- No data found on what happens administratively to camp infrastructure
  (solar plant, market infrastructure, shelters) as population continues to
  fall — is there a planned decommissioning/consolidation process, or is
  infrastructure simply being underutilized? Not addressed in any source
  reviewed.
- No disaggregated data on who is *not* returning (the residual population)
  — age, vulnerability status, disability, female-headed-household share
  among those remaining vs. those who returned — which is the single
  clearest need for understanding Zaatari's likely trajectory over the next
  1-2 years.

## 8. Draft ToC skeleton (for review, not final)

**Problem statement (draft):** Zaatari, built as durable infrastructure for
a protracted, growing displacement crisis, is now experiencing a rapid,
externally-triggered population decline (regional political shock, not a
managed decongestion program) that is occurring in lockstep with an
even faster contraction in humanitarian funding — leaving a residual,
harder-to-serve population exposed to eroding protection and economic
support just as international attention and resourcing shift toward the
returns narrative.

**Candidate long-term outcome:** The population remaining at Zaatari
through the return wave — disproportionately vulnerable households who are
not returning — retains access to protection, economic, and
disaster-preparedness capacity proportionate to their needs, rather than
proportionate to the shrinking headline camp population.

**Candidate intermediate outcomes (one per pathway):**
1. **Legal (A):** Clarity on Zaatari's medium-term status — is it winding
   down toward closure (as with the Emirati Jordanian Camp, Jul 2025), or
   persisting indefinitely at lower density? A published transition plan,
   rather than the current ambiguity, is the concrete target.
2. **Financing (B):** Funding allocated per-capita to the *residual*
   population rather than cut proportionate to whole-camp population
   decline — avoiding a scenario where the last, most vulnerable
   households receive the thinnest coverage.
3. **Disaster risk (C):** A documented JCD↔UNHCR/SRAD evacuation
   command structure and updated flood/fire risk mapping — independent of
   population trend, since the underlying hazards (winter storms, shelter
   fires) do not scale down with population.
4. **Protection (D):** Child protection and GBV case management capacity
   maintained at current or improved per-capita levels through the
   transition, with specific attention to child marriage drivers tied to
   economic precarity (Pathway B linkage documented above).

**Key assumptions to stress-test:**
- That the return wave continues at pace (UNHCR itself projects a wide
  range: 1-1.6 million Syria-wide returns in 2026) — a reversal or stall
  (e.g., due to conditions inside Syria) would change every pathway's
  urgency calculus. **Update, Sept 2026: this now looks like the wrong
  assumption to stress-test — the data suggests the opposite risk.**
  Jordan-specific returns are already decelerating (175,000 in 2025 vs. a
  3RP 2026 projection of only ~75,000, on pace as of Jun 2026's 74,000+
  YTD), attributed to intentions "spread over five years" plus limited
  services and security volatility inside Syria. The live risk is not a
  sudden reversal but a **long plateau of residual population** — exactly
  the scenario this file's problem statement already anticipates — at a
  slower, more predictable pace than the framing here assumed a month ago.
- That Zaatari's unique infrastructure maturity (solar grid feeding the
  national grid, formalized market) gives it *more* leverage than a typical
  camp to negotiate a managed transition — this is plausible but not
  evidenced in any source reviewed.
- That the residual population is meaningfully more vulnerable than the
  population that has already returned — a reasonable hypothesis given
  standard displacement-return dynamics, but not directly evidenced here
  (see Evidence Gaps).

**Suggested next research pass:** a dedicated look at who is *not*
returning and why — pulling IOM DTM or UNHCR's Enhanced Regional
Perceptions and Intentions Survey (ERPIS) microdata if accessible — would
convert this case from "situation analysis" to "intervention-ready" faster
than any other single research step, since it is the gap every candidate
intermediate outcome above ultimately depends on.
