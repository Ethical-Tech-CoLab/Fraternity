# Dzaleka Theory of Change — Situation Analysis

Broad-to-narrow situation research for a sector-level Theory of Change (ToC)
covering Dzaleka Refugee Camp, Malawi. Compiled via Tavily web research and
search, September 2026. **Not the ToC itself yet** — this is the evidence
base the ToC's problem tree, pathways, and assumptions should be built from.
Follows the same sourcing discipline as `camp-cost-indicators.md`: every
figure keeps its original source, date, and reporting period; nothing is
silently reconciled across conflicting numbers.

A note on the numbers: population and funding figures below diverge across
sources (e.g. 52,000 vs. 57,438 vs. 60,000+ for "current population"). This
reflects genuine reporting-period differences (UNHCR fact sheets, local
Dzaleka.com compilations, and media accounts are dated months apart), not an
error in this research. Always check the date attached to a figure before
using it.

## 2. Pathway A — Legal / policy reform

**Root structural driver.** Malawi's encampment policy rests on its
reservation to **Article 26 of the 1951 Refugee Convention** (freedom of
movement/choice of residence), treated as "recommendations only, not legally
binding." Domestic law is the **Refugees Act, 1989**, which does not itself
enumerate refugee rights — only status-determination procedure. [Dzaleka
encyclopedia — law and encampment
policy](https://services.dzaleka.com/encyclopedia/law-and-encampment-policy);
[research.unima.ac.mw thesis](https://research.unima.ac.mw/research-theses/theses/Makhola%20G.%202022.pdf)

| Event | Date | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ministry of Homeland Security "return to camp" order | Apr 2021 | All out-of-camp refugees ordered back to Dzaleka; ~8,000 people affected | [Refugee-Led Research Hub, "Pathways to Employment"](https://refugeeledresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/MALAWI.pdf) |
| Supreme Court injunction | 2021 | Blocked enforcement of the return order | Same source |
| High Court of Blantyre vacates injunction | Aug 2022 | Reopened path to forced relocation | Same source |
| Relocation deadlines | Nov 2022 (rural), Feb 2023 (urban) | Government-set deadlines | Same source |
| Mass arrests/detention | 17–18 May 2023 | 505 individuals (incl. 117 children) arrested, detained at Maula prison, forcibly relocated | [UNHCR press release](https://www.unhcr.org/news/press-releases/unhcr-warns-human-suffering-due-malawi-s-back-camp-refugee-policy); [HRW/ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/report/malawi/malawi-refugees-including-children-forcibly-relocated) |
| Judicial review filed (CHREAA) | 29 May 2023 | Challenges constitutionality of the detention/relocation; still pending in reviewed sources | [Southern Africa Litigation Centre](https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/protecting-the-rights-of-refugees-in-malawi) |
| Further directive, forced relocations with military assistance | Mar–Oct 2023 | 765 families / 2,296 people forcibly moved | [Global Detention Project](https://www.globaldetentionproject.org/malawis-encampment-policy-forced-relocations-and-detentions) |
| **National Implementation Plan on Migration (2025–2029) launched** | 29 Aug 2025, Lilongwe | Government + IOM; first pillar signaling a shift toward a rights-based migration framework | [migrationnetwork.un.org](https://migrationnetwork.un.org/malawi-launches-national-implementation-plan-global-compact-migration); [IOM Malawi](https://malawi.iom.int/resources/malawi-national-implementation-plan-gcm-2025-2029) |
| Revised Refugees Act under Special Law Commission review | 2025–2026 (ongoing) | Bill would address refugee status determination, rights/obligations, pathways to economic activity | [Malawi Nation — "Revised Refugees Act to address key gaps"](https://mwnation.com/revised-refugees-act-to-address-key-gaps); WFP engaged the Speaker of Parliament on the review |
| HRW: "swiftly implement commitments" | 18 Mar 2026 | Frames the gap as implementation speed, not political will — Malawi already endorsed the GCR/CRRF in 2018 | [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/03/18/malawi-swiftly-implement-commitments-address-human-rights-gaps) |

**Decongestion instruments tied to this pathway:**
- In-situ extension: Woodlot Area, Dzaleka Hills, New Katubza Area.
- **Kayilizi Open Settlement**, Chitipa District — 330 hectares approved by
  Parliament's Committee on Defence and Security; designed as an *open*
  settlement (refugees + host community share agricultural land), explicit
  pilot for the new rights-based legislation. **Estimated cost: US$90
  million; stuck at financing stage as of mid-2026.** [Malawi24, Jun
  2026](https://malawi24.com/2026/06/05/built-for-12000-holding-60000-overcrowded-dzaleka-camp-strains-malawis-refugee-system)
- UNHCR's own working ToC (2026 strategy, budget requirement **US$12.64M**):
  legal/policy reform → progressive transformation of camps into open
  settlements → self-reliance → durable solutions (local integration,
  repatriation, resettlement, complementary pathways). [UNHCR
  Malawi](https://www.unhcr.org/us/where-we-work/countries/malawi) — useful
  as a baseline/comparator against whatever pathway logic this ToC builds.

**Reform target, explicitly named:** the "**nine reservations**" barring
refugees' right to work, freedom of movement, and property ownership. Lifting
them is described as "the cornerstone of Malawi's commitment to the Global
Compact on Refugees." [dzaleka.com 2025 year in
review](https://www.dzaleka.com/2025/12/dzaleka-refugee-camp-2025-year-in-review.html)

## 3. Pathway B — Funding sustainability

**This is a global structural shock, not a Malawi-specific funding dip.**
UNHCR's 2026 Global Appeal: **US$8.505 billion** needed; the UN system-wide
"hyper-prioritized" ask for 2026 fell to **US$23 billion — the lowest since
2017** — following the US dismantling its aid department/budget in 2025.
[UNHCR Global Appeal 2026](https://www.unhcr.org/publications/global-appeal-2026);
[The New Humanitarian, "Five takeaways from the UN's aid plans for
2026"](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/news/2025/12/08/five-takeaways-uns-aid-plans-2026)

**Documented downstream causal chain** (not just correlation — explicitly
narrated across sources): funding cut → UNHCR downsizing (camp supervisors
and security personnel laid off, vehicles/equipment sold) → NGO withdrawal
(Plan International exited entirely in 2025, leaving child protection/GBV
gaps) → protection service vacuum → deteriorating protection outcomes (see
Pathway D). [The New
Humanitarian](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/opinion/first-person/2025/10/20/remember-we-exist-refugees-malawi-aid-slashed);
[dzaleka.com 2025
review](https://www.dzaleka.com/2025/12/dzaleka-refugee-camp-2025-year-in-review.html)

**Notable, worth independently verifying before citing further:** one source
(non-mainstream outlet) states Malawi is **no longer able to receive new
refugees** because UNHCR closed relevant registration offices, with arrivals
at the border being redirected toward Zambia. [The GOAT
PoL](https://thegoatpol.org/story/trump-decision-drawbacks-for-dzaleka) — flag
as **unverified/single-source** pending a UNHCR or ReliefWeb corroboration.

## 4. Pathway C — Disaster / evacuation risk

*(Most directly relevant to the ERCF/RICS evacuation-risk parameter work —
see `rics/PARAMETER_REGISTRY.md` and `rics/DESIGN.md` for where these feed
in.)*

| Hazard | Likelihood / impact assessment | Key evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flood | Credible, elevated — seasonal (Nov–Apr) heavy rains; DTM/orthomosaic and flood modeling already exist for the camp footprint | No public floodplain delineation or river-proximity dataset found — **genuine data gap** | [MDPI 2021 terrain study](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/9/1739); [CIMA Foundation Malawi Atlas Risk Profile](https://www.cimafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/MALAWI-ATLAS-RISK-PROFILE.pdf) |
| Fire | High likelihood, high impact — dense shelter layout + flammable materials (tarpaulin, bamboo); documented 2023–2024 "high regional camp fire incidence" | At least one concrete precedent event with an after-action report (site repair, shelter reconstruction, prepositioning) | [UNHCR fire incident report, Jan 2024](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2024-01/Microsoft%20Word%20-%202024.01.21_fire%20incident%20report_final.dotx_.pdf) |
| Disease — malaria | Endemic nationally, rising case counts 2023–2024 | Background/chronic risk, not an acute-event risk | [severemalaria.org](https://www.severemalaria.org/countries/malawi) |
| Disease — cholera | Actively surveilled; *V. cholerae* not detected in sampled pit latrines during a national outbreak (one QA sample positive) | Distinguishes "background risk" from "currently active risk" | [medRxiv, Jan 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.30.25321306v1) |
| Disease — COVID-19 | Recorded cases in camp, limited vaccination coverage | — | [malawi.un.org](https://malawi.un.org/en/download/74040/137480) |

**Referral/evacuation capacity constraints:**
- Primary health: Ministry of Health + UNHCR support, on-site.
- Referral: Dowa District Hospital (~10km); tertiary referral: Lilongwe
  (~45km). [UNHCR legacy
  PDF](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/legacy-pdf/4bff81589.pdf)
- Emergency services rated "critical" — limited ambulances, no night-time
  access, equipment shortages. [services.dzaleka.com/data](https://services.dzaleka.com/data)

**Institutional structure:**
- **DoDMA** (Department of Disaster Management Affairs) owns national
  early-warning and coordination — led the national response to Cyclone
  Freddy, March 2023. [ReliefWeb DoDMA sitrep
  #6](https://reliefweb.int/report/malawi/malawi-tropical-cyclone-freddy-department-disaster-management-affairs-dodma-situation-report-no-6-17-18-march-2023)
- **UNHCR/camp management** hold camp-level contingency plans and have
  engaged parametric risk-financing (ARC Replica insurance delivered drought
  payouts to Dzaleka households — see below).
- **The interface between the two is undocumented in open sources** — no
  published activation thresholds, command relationships, evacuation-route
  maps, or road/drainage condition assessments were found. This is flagged
  as **the single largest evidence gap for evacuation-risk modeling**.

**One concrete risk-financing precedent:** 4,000 households shared an
innovative insurance payout ($33/household/month) after El Niño-driven crop
failures — humanitarian groups had taken out parametric weather insurance.
[Wikipedia, citing 2024
reporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzaleka_Refugee_Camp)

**Cyclone Freddy (Mar 2023) — camp-level impact not confirmed.** National
DoDMA coordination and severe impacts in affected districts are documented;
no source in this research confirmed direct flooding impact to Dzaleka
specifically from that event. Do not assume camp-level impact without a
dedicated follow-up search.

## 6. Cross-pathway causal skeleton

```
[Funding collapse] ──┬──→ [NGO/UNHCR withdrawal] ──→ [Protection service vacuum] ──→ [GBV, trafficking, child protection risk ↑]
                      │                                        │
                      └──→ [Camp guard/security withdrawal] ──→ [Crime ↑ (+70% 2025→2026)] ──→ [erodes community trust/self-governance]

[Encampment policy / Art. 26 reservations] ──→ [Overcrowding locked in (~500% capacity)] ──→ [Disaster exposure ↑ (fire, flood, disease)]
                                                        │
                                                        └──→ [Referral/evacuation capacity constrained (10–45km to care)]

[Legal reform process (NIP 2025–2029, revised Refugees Act)] ──→ [Decongestion (Kayilizi / extensions)] ──→ [reduced overcrowding] ──→ [reduced disaster + protection risk]
        ▲
        └── bottlenecked by [Kayilizi financing gap: US$90M] ← itself downstream of [funding collapse]
```

**Reading:** the funding collapse (Pathway B) is the pivot node — it
independently degrades both the protection pathway (D) and, via
overcrowding lock-in, the disaster-risk pathway (C); it also stalls the one
structural fix (decongestion, under Pathway A) that would reduce both. Any
ToC built from this evidence should treat funding/financing mechanisms as a
candidate high-leverage intervention point, not just a resourcing footnote.

## 7. Evidence gaps (flagged, not filled)

- No reconciled population registry by date; household-size and
  registration-status breakdowns exist only at aggregate level.
- No public camp-level floodplain delineation, drainage map, or evacuation
  route map for Dzaleka.
- No documented activation thresholds or command relationship between DoDMA
  (national) and UNHCR/camp management (camp-level) for emergency evacuation.
- No disaggregated WASH/shelter/health facility capacity indicators (only
  qualitative "critical"/"congested" characterizations found).
- No consolidated camp management/coordination structure (cluster leads,
  refugee representative committees) documented in open sources.
- No donor-by-donor funding breakdown for Malawi specifically (global FTS
  data exists; Malawi-specific extraction not done in this pass).
- The "UNHCR can no longer register new arrivals / redirecting to Zambia"
  claim (Pathway B) is single-sourced and needs corroboration.
- No confirmed camp-level flood impact from Cyclone Freddy (2023) despite
  strong national-level documentation.

Recommended follow-up: UNHCR Malawi office, WFP Malawi country office, IOM
Malawi, DoDMA, and Inua Advocacy / Youth and Society (local NGOs quoted
repeatedly across sources) for primary data on the above.

## 8. Draft ToC skeleton (for review, not final)

**Problem statement (draft):** Dzaleka's encampment policy locks a rapidly
growing refugee population into a single, 500%-over-capacity site, while a
structural collapse in international humanitarian financing is
simultaneously withdrawing the protection and disaster-preparedness capacity
needed to manage that concentration risk — compounding both protection harms
(GBV, trafficking, crime) and disaster exposure (fire, flood, disease).

**Candidate long-term outcome:** Refugees and asylum seekers at Dzaleka live
in conditions where population density, legal status, and available
protection/emergency-response capacity are no longer structurally mismatched.

**Candidate intermediate outcomes (one per pathway):**
1. **Legal (A):** Nine reservations lifted / revised Refugees Act adopted →
   decongestion instruments (Kayilizi, extensions) financed and operational.
2. **Financing (B):** Diversified, less-earmarked funding base for
   UNHCR/WFP Malawi operations; Kayilizi's $90M gap closed.
3. **Disaster risk (C):** Camp-level evacuation plan with defined DoDMA↔UNHCR
   activation thresholds; floodplain/fire-risk mapping completed.
4. **Protection (D):** Dedicated child protection capacity restored; GBV/
   trafficking case management re-established; security patrol funding
   stabilized.

**Key assumptions to stress-test:**
- That legal reform (A) translates into *implemented* decongestion, not just
  statute — HRW's framing suggests implementation speed is the live risk.
- That new financing (B) would actually be allocated to Malawi given its low
  global visibility relative to Sudan/DRC/Ukraine.
- That decongestion (via Kayilizi) meaningfully reduces disaster risk (C)
  rather than merely relocating it to an undermapped new site.

**Suggested next research pass:** pick one pathway to move from "situation
analysis" to "intervention-ready" — e.g., a dedicated search for DoDMA↔UNHCR
camp evacuation SOPs (closes the largest gap in Pathway C, and is the
gap most directly actionable for the RICS/ERCF work).
