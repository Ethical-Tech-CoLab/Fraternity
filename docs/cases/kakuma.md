# Kakuma–Kalobeyei Theory of Change — Situation Analysis

Broad-to-narrow situation research for a sector-level Theory of Change (ToC)
covering Kakuma Refugee Camp and the adjacent Kalobeyei Integrated
Settlement, Turkana West, Kenya. Compiled via Tavily web research, September
2026. **Not the ToC itself yet** — this is the evidence base the ToC's
problem tree, pathways, and assumptions should be built from. Follows the
same sourcing discipline as `dzaleka.md`: every figure keeps its original
source, date, and reporting period; nothing is silently reconciled across
conflicting numbers.

A note on the numbers: population figures below diverge by a few thousand
depending on source and cutoff date (e.g. 309,169 vs. 309,867 vs. 310,755
for "system population, late 2025") — this reflects genuine reporting-cycle
differences between Kenya's Refugee Affairs Secretariat statistics package,
UNHCR data portal snapshots, and NGO compilations, not an error in this
research.

**Refreshed 2026-09-22** after a Tavily API-key fix (a prior key had gone
stale in one local config file); re-ran research across all four pathways
to confirm sourcing still resolves and to catch anything changed since the
original September 2026 compile. Updates from this pass are flagged inline
as "Update, Sept 2026." Nothing in the original file was found to be
wrong; the most material additions are a fuller 2025–2026 WFP ration
timeline (including a partial recovery in 2026 not visible in the
original pass) and Shirika Plan implementation detail that partially, not
fully, closes this file's largest previously-flagged gap.

## 2. Pathway A — Legal / policy reform

**Structural framing differs sharply from Dzaleka.** Kenya is not defending
a treaty reservation against reform — it has already legislated a reform
(**Refugees Act, 2021**) and is now in an implementation phase, with
**implementing regulations published 2024** and the **Shirika Plan**
(developed with government + partners, e.g. GIZ) explicitly aiming to shift
from camp-based humanitarian assistance toward socioeconomic integration
aligned with national development planning. [refugee.go.ke — Shirika
Plan](https://refugee.go.ke/kenya-shirika-plan); [Refugees International —
"Lessons and recommendations for implementing Kenya's new refugee
law"](https://refugeesinternational.org/reports-briefs/lessons-and-recommendations-for-implementing-kenyas-new-refugee-law);
[GIZ Shirika support
project](https://giz.de/en/projects/support-implementation-shirika-plan-strengthening-socioeconomic-integration-refugees-and)

| Event | Date | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Refugee Act enacted | 2021 | National law establishing Refugee Affairs Secretariat (RAS), formal legal status framework | [Refugees International](https://refugeesinternational.org/reports-briefs/lessons-and-recommendations-for-implementing-kenyas-new-refugee-law) |
| RAS assumes camp management/protection roles from UNHCR | 2016 (predates the Act; context for "encamped states" governance structure) | Kenyan state agency formally took over key camp managerial and protection positions | [Encamped States thesis, MLU Halle-Wittenberg](https://www.academia.edu/88459009/Encamped_States_The_State_of_the_Camp_in_Kakuma_Refugee_Camp_and_Kalobeyei_Integrated_Settlement) |
| Refugees (General) Regulations published | 2024 | Implementing regulations operationalizing the 2021 Act | [UNHCR RIMAP](https://rimap.unhcr.org/node/295) |
| Supreme Court advisory opinion, *Kituo Cha Sheria v. Attorney General* | 12 Apr 2024 | Judicial engagement with refugee-rights questions; **decision text and practical effect on Kakuma-level encampment policy not established in this research pass** | [Kenya Law](https://kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kesc/2024/15/eng@2024-04-12) |
| Shirika Plan development ongoing | 2024–2026 | Government + GIZ + partners; climate-action and economic-inclusion pillars explicitly named | [refugee.go.ke](https://refugee.go.ke/kenya-shirika-plan); [GIZ](https://giz.de/en/projects/support-implementation-shirika-plan-strengthening-socioeconomic-integration-refugees-and) |

**Evidence gap, flagged explicitly:** unlike Dzaleka (where forced-relocation
events, court injunctions, and mass-arrest dates are well documented),
**no site-level documentation was found in this pass of what freedom of
movement or right-to-work looks like in practice at Kakuma** post-2021 —
i.e., whether the Act's rights are actually exercised on the ground, or
remain a paper reform awaiting Shirika implementation. This is the single
largest Pathway A gap and should be the first target of a follow-up search.

**Update, Sept 2026 — this gap is now partially closing.** A year-plus of
Shirika implementation data exists that was not surfaced in the original
pass: the Plan is costed at **~US$943 million** across three phases
(transition 2024–2027, stabilisation 2028–2031, resilience 2032–2035;
Kenya government + World Bank + donors + private-sector financiers), and
two donor-funded projects are explicitly **Kakuma/Turkana-site-specific**
— Germany's joint GIZ–UNHCR–Department of Refugee Services project
(launched to support Shirika implementation directly in Turkana) and
Denmark's Inclusive Refugee Response Programme (IRRP, funded via the
Danish MFA + Grundfos/Novo Nordisk/LEGO foundations), both explicitly
named as working "in Turkana County and the Kakuma refugee camp." A
USCRI one-year-on statement (27 Mar 2026) still frames the Plan as a
"promise" rather than delivered outcomes. Concretely, on-the-ground
implementation looks mixed rather than absent: refugees can, in theory,
register for mobile money using refugee ID cards since mid-2025, but "not
all branches of telecom companies... accept the cards, despite the
government's ruling" (The New Humanitarian, Jul 2025) — a small, dated,
site-level data point of exactly the kind flagged as missing. The same
source documents local host-community opposition and warns refugee
integration risks becoming "a political football" ahead of Kenya's 2027
elections. [USCRI, "One Year On," 27 Mar
2026](https://refugees.org/statement-one-year-on-advancing-the-promise-of-kenyas-shirika-plan);
[UNHCR Kenya, Germany–GIZ Shirika
project](https://www.unhcr.org/ke/news/press-releases/germany-kenya-and-unhcr-launch-joint-project-support-implementation-shirika-1);
[Denmark IRRP,
LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/world-refugee-day-2026-kenyas-shirika-plan-rethinking-sqqef);
[The New Humanitarian, Jul
2025](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/analysis/2025/07/21/kenyas-flagship-refugee-integration-plan-runs-local-opposition)

## 3. Pathway B — Funding sustainability

**Same global structural shock as Dzaleka (Pathway B there), landing on a
much larger population.** UNHCR Kenya's funding requirement and shortfall
sit inside the same 2025–2026 global aid contraction documented in the
Dzaleka case file (UNHCR 2026 Global Appeal $8.505B; UN system-wide ask
fell to $23B, lowest since 2017).

| Indicator | Value | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| UNHCR Kenya funding requirement, 2024 | $165.9M | 2024 | [UNHCR Kenya ARR 2024](https://unhcr.org/mx/sites/default/files/2025-06/Kenya%20ARR%202024.pdf) |
| UNHCR Kenya funded share | ~52% of needs-based budget | 2024 | Same |
| UNHCR global voluntary-contribution shortfall | ~$425M below prior-year pace | early Jul 2024 | [UNHCR — Underfunded 2024](https://unhcr.org/publications/underfunded-report-2024) |
| WFP ration cut | Reduced to ~40% of minimum food basket | Feb–May 2024 | [WFP news release](https://wfp.org/news/refugees-kenya-risk-worsening-hunger-wfp-faces-critical-funding-shortfall) |
| WFP food distribution — **fully suspended** (indefinite) | Announced 8 Aug 2025, citing insecurity risk to staff/refugees; >70% of Kakuma/Kalobeyei refugees left without food aid; local leaders reported ≥5 deaths, ~12 injuries in resulting unrest | Aug 2025 | [SOS Médias Burundi](https://www.sosmediasburundi.org/en/2025/08/08/kakuma-and-kalobeyei-kenya-food-aid-suspended-the-refugees-anger-mounts) |
| Emergency US donor top-up | $37M | 2024 | [ReliefWeb — WFP welcomes US$37M](https://reliefweb.int/report/kenya/wfp-welcomes-us37-million-us-government-support-refugees-kenya-and-moves-prioritise-food-assistance-most-vulnerable) |
| New "differentiated assistance" vulnerability-based policy | Implemented early Jul 2025 by UNHCR/WFP jointly | 2025 | [SOS Médias Burundi](https://www.sosmediasburundi.org/en/2025/08/08/kakuma-and-kalobeyei-kenya-food-aid-suspended-the-refugees-anger-mounts) |

**Notable divergence from Dzaleka:** the Aug 2025 WFP suspension is framed
as insecurity-driven (protecting staff), layered on top of the
funding-driven ration cuts — a compounding cause not present in the
Dzaleka file, where the funding-withdrawal chain runs cleanly
funding→drawdown→vacuum without a self-reinforcing unrest loop feeding
back into the suspension decision itself. That feedback loop (ration
cut → protest/unrest → deaths → *further* suspension) is worth testing
as a distinct causal pattern in the cross-case comparison.

**Downstream effects reported (2024–2025), same shape as Dzaleka Pathway
B→D chain:** reduced water allocations, suspended cash transfers, reduced
fuel/field capacity, service reductions across health/education/protection,
and reported increases in protection caseloads per caseworker. [ReliefWeb —
"Kenya refugee response under strain," Aug
2025](https://reliefweb.int/report/kenya/kenya-refugee-response-under-strain-funding-cuts-differentiated-assistance-and-rising-social-cohesion-crisis-august-2025)

**Update, Sept 2026 — the fuller ration timeline, and a partial 2026
recovery not visible in the original pass:** the ration collapse was
worse, and more volatile, than the single Aug 2025 suspension figure
suggested: WFP served ~720,000 refugees across Dadaab, Kakuma and
Kalobeyei as of May 2025; cut rations to 40% (Feb 2025), then a record
low of **28% of the minimum food basket** by June 2025 with cash assistance
halted entirely (WFP appealed for $44M to restore full rations through
August 2025); by August 2025, the Differentiated Assistance model reached
only 69% of the population (Category 1/2 households) at 40%/20% of the
food basket. Kalobeyei saw a violent protest on **28 July 2025** tied to
this collapse. A dated, direct population effect: **9,300+ South Sudanese
refugees left Kakuma/Kalobeyei to walk back to South Sudan between June
and 11 Sept 2025**, nearly two-thirds of them children — many sent ahead
alone by parents who could not afford transport for the whole family, a
distinct family-separation mechanism this file had not captured.
**Partial recovery in 2026:** WFP's Feb 2026 Kenya Country Brief confirms
rations were raised again for the Feb–Sept 2026 window, to **80%** for
Category 1 and **60%** for Category 2 households — better than the Aug
2025 low, though still below full rations, and this file's Pathway D
protection findings (crime, social tension) were generated under the
worse 2025 conditions, not yet re-tested against the 2026 partial
recovery. [WFP, "Refugees in Kenya at risk of worsening hunger," May
2025](https://www.wfp.org/news/refugees-kenya-risk-worsening-hunger-wfp-faces-critical-funding-shortfall);
[WFP Kenya Country Brief, Aug
2025](https://reliefweb.int/report/kenya/wfp-kenya-country-brief-august-2025);
[WFP Kenya Country Brief, Feb
2026](https://docs.wfp.org/api/documents/WFP-0000172572/download);
[Global Health Justice, citing UNHCR 11 Sept 2025
update](https://depts.washington.edu/globalhealthjustice/aid-funding-cuts-and-malnutrition-force-kakuma-refugees-back-to-south-sudan)

## 4. Pathway C — Disaster / evacuation risk

Unlike Dzaleka (flood risk "credible but undocumented at incident level"),
**Kakuma has multiple confirmed, dated flood and disease-outbreak events** —
this pathway is evidentially richer here, though the institutional
evacuation/referral-capacity question remains as much a gap as at Dzaleka.

| Hazard | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Flood — dam break, re-displacement | April 2024 dam breaks re-displaced **>23,000 refugees from Dadaab and Kakuma combined** (Kakuma-specific count not isolated in this source) | [Wilson Center, citing IDMC](https://gbv.wilsoncenter.org/article/catastrophic-flooding-and-its-impact-displaced-people-chad) |
| Flood — Turkana-wide | "Significant flooding" in Turkana County affecting Kakuma refugees, cited in global displacement reporting | [IDMC Global Report on Internal Displacement 2024](https://disasterdisplacement.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/IDMC-GRID-2024-Global-Report-on-Internal-Displacement-2.pdf) |
| Flood — 2024/25 rains, agricultural destruction | Record-breaking rains after a record drought inundated the region; crops, seeds, tools, latrines destroyed; first-hand refugee interviews (Apr 2025) describe total loss of livelihoods; **no environmental early-warning system or anticipatory response found** — described as a structural planning failure, not a one-off event | [ODI Humanitarian Practice Network, Jun 2025](https://odihpn.org/en/publication/between-drought-and-deluge-the-fragility-of-resilience-in-kakuma-refugee-camp) |
| Cholera — historical baseline | First recorded outbreak 2005; 2017 outbreak (Kakuma/Kalobeyei/Dadaab clustered, 76 deaths across 20 counties nationally, CFR 1.9%); 2017–18 study found Kalobeyei incidence (1.33/1000) ~5x Kakuma's (0.28/1000); DRC-origin residents at ~9x, South Sudan-origin at ~7x relative risk vs. Somalia-origin | [PMC cross-sectional study, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7813661) |
| Cholera — active outbreak, 2025 | 74 cases (30 lab-confirmed), 1 fatality, first case reported 18 May 2025, county emergency response briefing held 6 Jun 2025 | [Turkana County Government sitrep](https://turkana.go.ke/2025/06/09/cholera-situation-report-update-in-kakuma-and-kalobeyei-settlements) |
| Cholera — further outbreak reported | 22 Nov 2025 | [SOS Médias Burundi headline reference](https://www.sosmediasburundi.org/en/2025/08/08/kakuma-and-kalobeyei-kenya-food-aid-suspended-the-refugees-anger-mounts) — **flagged: headline-only reference picked up in a news index; not independently verified against a primary sitrep in this pass** |

**Institutional structure — same evidence gap shape as Dzaleka:**
- Turkana County Government operates a **County Health Rapid Response Team
  (CHRRT)**, confirmed active for the Jun 2025 cholera response.
- No published activation thresholds, county-level Disaster Risk Management
  (DRM) office ↔ UNHCR/RAS camp-management command relationship,
  evacuation-route maps, or flood-early-warning system were found —
  the ODI HPN piece explicitly names the **absence of an early-warning
  system** as a structural failure, not merely an unresearched gap.
- **This is the same "undocumented DoDMA-equivalent ↔ camp-management
  interface" gap flagged at Dzaleka** — suggesting it may be a pattern
  across protracted refugee settings generally, not a Malawi- or
  Kenya-specific evidence gap. Worth testing explicitly once a third case
  is added.

**Update, Sept 2026:** a peer-reviewed systems-failure analysis (STAMP-CAST
methodology, Aug 2026) of cholera endemicity specifically at Kakuma and
Dadaab is now published — the first academic evidence in this file's
research that formally frames the institutional-interface gap as a
*systems* failure rather than a resourcing gap, corroborating the pattern
flagged above. Separately, the drought-to-flood whiplash documented in the
Apr 2025 ODI piece is not a one-off: Kenya declared a fresh flood
emergency in **March 2026** (CARE, 27 Mar 2026) layered on continuing
drought in the arid/semi-arid counties, with 3.7 million people projected
into IPC Phase 3+ acute food insecurity Apr–Jun 2026, and a further strong
El Niño forecast for the 2026/2027 season (also flagged in the Dzaleka
case file's Pathway B, §3, as a cross-regional risk). Camp-level flash
flooding in Kakuma's seasonal river (laga) continued to be reported
informally through 2026, consistent with the historical pattern (river
merger/erosion threat first documented as far back as 2003). [Pan African
Medical Journal / STAMP-CAST, Aug
2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-026-28948-z);
[CARE, "Kenya now faces both flood emergency and drought crisis," 27 Mar
2026](https://reliefweb.int/report/kenya/kenya-now-faces-both-flood-emergency-and-drought-crisis-country-braces-cholera-risk)

this pass; protection findings here are qualitative/programmatic rather
than incident-count-based. A Kenya Police/RAS security-incident dataset
should be the target of a follow-up search, to make this pathway
comparable in kind to the Dzaleka file's Pathway D table.

**Update, Sept 2026:** still no incident-count dataset, but new
qualitative/programmatic evidence fills in around it. OCHA's Financial
Tracking Service shows the Danish Refugee Council holds a dated, costed

"life-saving protection to at risk refugees in Nairobi, Dadaab, Kakuma and

camp found in this research. Two new documented harms, neither in the
original pass: a peer-reviewed/practitioner analysis (CSNS, Jan 2026) on

[USCRI, "Recovering Childhood"

## 6. Cross-pathway causal skeleton

```
[Global funding collapse] ──┬──→ [WFP ration cuts → full suspension, Aug 2025] ──→ [Unrest/protests (≥5 deaths)] ──┐
                             │                                                                                      │
                             │                                                            (feedback loop) ◄─────────┘
                             │
                             ├──→ [UNHCR/NGO service drawdown] ──→ [Child protection capacity collapses first] ──→ [Abuse, FGM, early marriage, trafficking risk ↑]
                             │
                             └──→ [No resourcing for climate early-warning] ──→ [2024/25 floods hit with zero anticipatory response] ──→ [Livelihoods destroyed, disease risk ↑ (cholera)]

[Refugee Act 2021 + Shirika Plan] ──→ [Stated shift: camp → integration] ──→ [Implementation status at site level: UNDOCUMENTED — largest Pathway A gap]
```

**Reading:** as at Dzaleka, funding collapse (Pathway B) is the pivot node
— but at Kakuma it produces an additional, self-reinforcing loop (ration
cuts → unrest → further suspension) not documented at Dzaleka. Legal
reform here is *ahead* of Dzaleka's (a law already passed, vs. a
reservation Malawi is still defending) — yet the practical, site-level
effect of that head start is undocumented in open sources, which is
itself a notable finding: **legislative progress does not by itself close
the evidence gap on lived rights**.

## 7. Evidence gaps (flagged, not filled)

- No site-level documentation of freedom-of-movement or right-to-work
  *practice* at Kakuma post-2021 Refugee Act — only the legislative/policy
  layer is documented.
- No isolated Kakuma-only flood-displacement count (the 23,000 figure is
  Dadaab+Kakuma combined).
- No published DRM (county) ↔ RAS/UNHCR camp-management activation
  thresholds or evacuation-route mapping — same gap shape as Dzaleka.
- No dated, quantified crime-rate trend specific to Kakuma/Turkana West.
- The 22 Nov 2025 cholera-outbreak reference is headline-only and
  unverified against a primary sitrep.
- No donor-by-donor funding breakdown specific to Kakuma/Kalobeyei (vs.
  Kenya-wide).
- No combined (Kakuma+Kalobeyei) design-capacity figure from a primary
  source; the ~239% over-capacity ratio in §1 is derived, not sourced
  directly.

Recommended follow-up: UNHCR Kenya operational dashboards, Turkana County
DRM office incident logs, RAS registration/movement directives, DRC Kenya's
ongoing Protection Monitoring Risks and Trends series (quarterly), and a
Kenya Police Service Turkana West crime-data request.

## 8. Draft ToC skeleton (for review, not final)

**Problem statement (draft):** Kakuma-Kalobeyei hosts over 300,000 people at
roughly 2.4–3.4x combined design capacity, in a region with no climate
early-warning infrastructure, while a global humanitarian-funding collapse
is simultaneously cutting food assistance to the point of triggering fatal
unrest and stripping child-protection capacity first — even as Kenya's own
legal framework (Refugee Act 2021, Shirika Plan) points toward integration
rather than continued encampment.

**Candidate long-term outcome:** Refugees and asylum seekers at
Kakuma-Kalobeyei live under a legal status, funding base, and
disaster-preparedness capacity no longer structurally mismatched to
population scale — mirroring the Dzaleka candidate outcome, suggesting this
framing may generalize across cases.

**Candidate intermediate outcomes (one per pathway):**
1. **Legal (A):** Refugee Act 2021 / Shirika Plan commitments translate into
   documented, site-level freedom-of-movement and right-to-work practice —
   closing the implementation-evidence gap is itself an intermediate
   outcome, not just a research task.
2. **Financing (B):** Diversified, less-earmarked funding restores full
   WFP rations without a fatal-unrest→further-cut feedback loop.
3. **Disaster risk (C):** A functioning climate/flood early-warning system
   and a documented DRM↔camp-management activation protocol exist.
4. **Protection (D):** Child-protection capacity is resourced
   independently of general funding swings, given its documented
   first-to-collapse position.

**Key assumptions to stress-test:**
- That Kenya's *legislative* head start (vs. Malawi's still-contested
  reservation) actually produces faster *practical* change — untested in
  this research pass.
- That the Aug 2025 unrest-suspension feedback loop is a one-off, not a
  recurring dynamic that will repeat with the next funding shock.
- That Shirika's integration framing is compatible with, rather than a
  distraction from, urgent disaster-preparedness needs identified by ODI
  HPN.

**Suggested next research pass:** verify whether the "undocumented
DRM↔camp-management interface" gap found at both Dzaleka and Kakuma is a
genuine cross-case pattern (candidate high-leverage, low-cost
intervention: publishing/formalizing an existing informal interface) or
an artifact of this research method's blind spots — the next case file
should test this specifically before it's treated as a finding.
