# Bidibidi Theory of Change — Situation Analysis

Broad-to-narrow situation research for a sector-level Theory of Change (ToC)
covering Bidibidi Refugee Settlement, Yumbe District, Uganda. Compiled via
Tavily web research, September 2026. **Not the ToC itself yet** — this is
the evidence base the ToC's problem tree, pathways, and assumptions should
be built from. Follows the sourcing discipline set out in `../../CONCEPT.md`:
every figure keeps its original source, date, and reporting period; nothing
is silently reconciled across conflicting numbers.

A note on the numbers: Bidibidi's reported population has fallen sharply
across 2025–2026 sources (>270,000 in Jun 2025 vs. 209,783 on 28 Feb 2026).
This may reflect genuine repatriation/relocation, or it may reflect
biometric re-verification correcting an inflated historical count — Uganda
ran a contested biometric re-registration in Bidibidi in mid-2018, and no
source in this pass disentangles the two mechanisms for the 2025–2026
decline. Flagged as an evidence gap below, not resolved here.

**Refreshed 2026-09-22** after a Tavily API-key fix; re-ran research across
all four pathways. The population-decline mechanism (this file's single
most consequential open question) is still not resolved, but this pass
found a direct signal that UNHCR itself treats it as needing explanation,
not as a routine data update — see §1 update below. The most concrete new
finding is that Yumbe/Bidibidi-specific quarterly sector dashboards (Child
Protection, GBV, Health & Nutrition, WASH, Shelter, Environment & Energy,
Cash/Voucher Assistance) now exist for Q1–Q2 2026, which could close
several of this file's "no Bidibidi-specific data" gaps in a follow-up
pass — their contents were not extracted in this pass, only their
existence confirmed. Updates flagged inline as "Update, Sept 2026."

## 2. Pathway A — Legal / policy

**Structural context is the inverse of Dzaleka's.** Uganda's **Refugees
Act, 2006** (operational 2009) and **Refugees Regulations, 2010** grant
refugees freedom of movement (Section 30, restricted only by the same
general rules that apply to foreigners — national security, public order,
public health/morals), the right to work, to own/lease property, and to
register companies on the same terms as citizens (Companies Act 2012,
Partnerships Act 2010). This is internationally cited as one of the most
progressive refugee legal frameworks in the world. [World Bank, "Uganda's
Pioneer Refugee
Model"](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/e0a2fc1f-f982-42f6-8c0d-32fecaae3140/download);
[IRC policy
review](https://rebuild.rescue.org/sites/default/files/2022-11/IRC%20Kenya%20and%20Uganda%20Refugee%20Policy%20and%20Practice%20Review%20-%209th%20June%202022%20%281%29.pdf)

| Element | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Governance model | Settlement Transformation Agenda (STA) + Comprehensive Refugee Response Framework (CRRF); six pillars: land management, sustainable livelihoods, governance, peaceful coexistence, environmental protection, community empowerment | [SIHMA](https://sihma.org.za/Blog-on-the-move/ugandas-refugee-friendly-policies-ugandas-progressive-approach-to-refugee-self-reliance?lang=en) |
| Land allocation model | 30m × 30m (900 m²) plot per household, fixed since the policy became operational in 2009, **regardless of household size** | [Refugees International, "From Promise to Practice," Jun 2026](https://www.refugeesinternational.org/reports-briefs/from-promise-to-practice-delivering-on-ugandas-refugee-model) |
| Land base status, 2026 | Population at Uganda scale grew ~10x since 2009; land base "exhausted" — new arrivals receive slivers of already-occupied land | Same |
| Refugee governance structure | Elected Refugee Welfare Councils (RWC1 settlement / RWC2 cluster / RWC3 zone), overseen by OPM | [UNHCR/Contested Refuge report](https://data.unhcr.org/en/documents/download/66344) |
| Implementation gap flagged | Section 44(2) of the Act "has sometimes been" used to restrict movement in practice despite Section 30's guarantee — a documented de jure/de facto gap | [IRC policy review](https://rebuild.rescue.org/sites/default/files/2022-11/IRC%20Kenya%20and%20Uganda%20Refugee%20Policy%20and%20Practice%20Review%20-%209th%20June%202022%20%281%29.pdf) |
| National development integration | Fourth National Development Plan (NDP IV, 2025/26–2029/30) aligns refugee-hosting district plans with national priorities; ILO/National Planning Authority Employment Mainstreaming Action Plan finalized Mar 2025 | [ILO, 2026](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/Uganda_GPs_Final%20Format_190626.pdf) |
| World Bank financing | Oct 2025: Uganda's Parliament approved US$1.341B in World Bank borrowing across five projects; US$180.5M to DRDIP II (Development Response to Displacement Impacts Project), a second $152.27M credit pending — ~$330M total from the IDA Window for Host Communities and Refugees | [Refugees International](https://www.refugeesinternational.org/reports-briefs/from-promise-to-practice-delivering-on-ugandas-refugee-model) |

**Reform target, implicitly named:** unlike Dzaleka (where the legal
framework itself is the binding constraint), Bidibidi's legal framework is
already rights-based — the gap is **implementation catching up with a
10x scale increase**, specifically the fixed land-allocation formula. This
reframes what "Pathway A" leverage looks like relative to Dzaleka/Kakuma.

**Update, Sept 2026:** the elected-governance structure (RWC1/2/3) is
actively functioning, not just formally documented — refugees in Bidibidi
voted for internal Refugee Welfare Council leadership on **18 August
2026**, a concrete, dated instance of the governance model in current
operation. [The Borderlines, via
Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/theborderlines001/posts/refugees-in-ugandas-bidibidi-settlement-are-voting-for-their-internal-leadership/122202731480873065)

year** — despite "the generous support of donors," the gap persists. This
is broadly consistent with (not a replacement for) the $47M-through-2026
and $75M-for-2026 figures found elsewhere in WFP's own reporting, which
likely reflect different scopes/dates within the same funding picture
rather than a contradiction; kept side by side, not reconciled. [WFP USA,

**Institutional structure — notably different from Dzaleka.** Uganda's
**Ministry of Relief, Disaster Preparedness and Refugees** (within the
Office of the Prime Minister, under Hon. Sam Engola) combines national
disaster management **and** refugee management under a single ministry —
unlike Dzaleka's split between Malawi's DoDMA (disaster) and UNHCR/camp
management (refugees), which the Dzaleka case file flagged as an
undocumented interface. The **Department of Relief, Disaster Preparedness
and Management (DRPM)** runs the **National Emergency Coordination and
Operations Centre (NECOC)**, and Uganda has a **National Roadmap on
Anticipatory Action 2026–2031** — a proactive-response framework. [OPM
Uganda](https://opm.go.ug/disaster-preparedness-management); [Uganda
Anticipatory Action Roadmap
2026](https://www.icpac.net/documents/1098/UGANDA_ANTICIPATORY_ACTION_ROADMAP_2026_90X0W0U.pdf)

**Caveat:** institutional unification (one ministry for both functions) is
not the same as a confirmed, published, Bidibidi-specific evacuation plan
or activation threshold — no such site-level document was found in this
pass (see Evidence gaps).

**Mechanistic link to Pathway B (funding), documented directly in
sources:** funding cut → WFP ration cuts/categorization → malnutrition
(GAM tripled in Bidibidi) → suicide risk, school dropout, child neglect —
a cleaner, more directly narrated single chain than Dzaleka's multi-hop
protection cascade. **Separately**, the firewood/GBV dynamic is
**not** funding-driven — it traces to the fixed 900 m² land-allocation
model (Pathway A) not scaling with population, which is a distinct causal
route from the funding-collapse pathway.

## 6. Cross-pathway causal skeleton

```
[Funding collapse] ──→ [WFP ration cuts + vulnerability categorization] ──→ [GAM more than tripled in Bidibidi] ──→ [suicide risk ↑, school dropout ↑, child neglect ↑]
                                          │
                                          └──→ [TSFP discontinued, 7 locations] ──→ [maternal/child nutrition risk ↑]

[Land allocation fixed at 900m²/household since 2009] ──→ [land base exhausted after ~10x population growth] ──→ [refugees forage host (Aringa) land for firewood, no alt. fuel provided]
                                                                                                                        │
                                                                                                                        └──→ [host-refugee friction + GBV risk to women collecting firewood] ──→ [erodes CRRF "peaceful coexistence" pillar]

[Refugees Act 2006 / Refugee Regulations 2010 — already rights-based (freedom of movement, right to work)] ──→ [NOT the binding constraint here, unlike Dzaleka] ──→ [de facto gap is land-allocation scaling + labour-market absorption, not the statute itself]
```

**Reading:** unlike Dzaleka, where legal/policy reform is the stalled
upstream lever, Bidibidi's legal framework is not the constraint — the
funding collapse (B) and a *scaling failure in land allocation* (a policy
**implementation** gap under A, not a policy **design** gap) are the two
independent pivot nodes. They converge on protection outcomes (D) via two
different mechanisms: malnutrition/mental-health (funding-driven) and
GBV/host-conflict (land-scaling-driven). Any ToC built from this evidence
should treat these as two separate levers, not one combined "funding
fixes everything" narrative.

## 7. Evidence gaps (flagged, not filled)

- **Population decline mechanism unconfirmed.** Reported population fell
  from >270,000 (Jun 2025) to 209,783 (28 Feb 2026) — no source
  distinguishes genuine repatriation/relocation from correction of an
  inflated count via biometric re-verification (Uganda ran a contested
  biometric exercise in Bidibidi in mid-2018; tension over it is
  documented, but no 2025–2026 re-verification event was confirmed).
- **Disease surveillance capacity data is stale** — the only public IDSR
  evaluation found dates to March 2017; no 2024–2026 reassessment located.
- **No Bidibidi-specific evacuation plan, shelter inventory, or activation
  threshold found**, despite Uganda's disaster-management and
  refugee-management functions sitting under one ministry (OPM) — this
  organizational unification is not evidence that a site-level SOP for
  Bidibidi exists; it was simply not found in this pass.
- **No disaggregated, dated crime/security statistics for Bidibidi**
  specifically (contrast Dzaleka's Dowa Police figures or Kakuma's
  insecurity-linked WFP suspension) — protection data here is qualitative
  (UNESCO risk-type list, adolescent-pregnancy count) rather than a
  trend series.
- **No confirmed camp-level flood or fire incident log** — a
  district-level climate hazard assessment exists, but no dated
  Bidibidi-specific disaster event history (contrast Dzaleka's Jan 2024
  fire incident report, Za'atari's GIS flood layer).
- **Labour-market outcomes not quantified for Bidibidi specifically** —
  national/cross-settlement research (Betts et al., Refugee Studies
  Centre) documents the self-reliance model's effects in aggregate, but
  no Bidibidi-only income/employment figures were found.
- **Update, Sept 2026 — several of the above may be resolvable, not
  genuinely absent:** this pass located a series of **Yumbe Sub-Office
  quarterly UCRRP Settlement Performance Snapshots** (Q1 and Q2 2026
  confirmed) plus Q2 2026 national sector dashboards covering Child
  Protection, GBV, Health & Nutrition, WASH, Shelter/Settlement/NFI, and
  Environment & Energy — all on UNHCR's Uganda Operational Data Portal.
  These are exactly the kind of site-level, dated, sector-specific sources
  that would close the crime/security, disease-surveillance, and
  labour-market gaps above. This pass confirmed they exist but did not
  extract their contents — the highest-value, most concrete next step for
  this file, more actionable than "no data exists." [UNHCR Uganda
  ODP](https://data.unhcr.org/en/country/uga)

## 8. Draft ToC skeleton (for review, not final)

**Problem statement (draft):** Bidibidi hosts refugees under one of the
world's most legally progressive refugee frameworks, yet a decade of
population growth against a land-allocation model fixed at founding-era
terms has exhausted the settlement's land base, while a parallel collapse
in international humanitarian financing has cut food assistance to record
lows — producing acute malnutrition, documented mental-health harm
(rising suicide), and GBV risk tied to resource competition, even though
the underlying legal/policy structure is not itself the binding
constraint.

**Candidate long-term outcome:** Refugees at Bidibidi achieve food
security and reduced resource-competition conflict with the host
community, sustained by financing and land allocation that scale with
actual population rather than remaining fixed at 2009-era assumptions.

**Candidate intermediate outcomes (one per pathway):**
1. **Legal (A):** Land-allocation formula and labour-market-access
   provisions revisited to reflect ~10x population growth since 2009,
   closing the de jure/de facto implementation gap.
2. **Financing (B):** Diversified, less donor-concentrated funding
   restores WFP rations above the Category-2 (22%) floor and reinstates
   TSFP in the 7 discontinued locations.
3. **Disaster risk (C):** A published, Bidibidi-specific evacuation/
   emergency-response plan and an updated (post-2017) disease-surveillance
   capacity assessment.
4. **Protection (D):** Alternative cooking fuel provided to reduce GBV
   exposure during firewood collection; adolescent-pregnancy and
   suicide-prevention programming scaled to match documented incidence.

**Key assumptions to stress-test:**
- That the population decline (270k → 210k) reflects reduced genuine need
  rather than an undercount masking continued strain — this materially
  changes how per-capita funding/land figures should be read.
- That because Uganda's legal framework is not the binding constraint
  (unlike Dzaleka), legal-pathway intervention is lower-priority here —
  worth testing specifically against the land-allocation implementation
  gap, which is a legal/policy-administration issue, not a protection or
  funding one.
- That firewood/GBV dynamics are addressable via alternative-fuel
  provision alone, rather than requiring resolution of the underlying
  land-rights ambiguity between refugees and the Aringa host community.

**Suggested next research pass:** confirm the population-decline mechanism
(repatriation vs. re-verification) via UNHCR Uganda's monthly Active
Population by Settlement series and any 2025–2026 biometric
re-registration announcement — this is the single most consequential
unresolved number in this case file, since it determines whether Bidibidi
is a "successful decongestion" story or a "population undercount" story.
