# Cross-case comparison

First cross-case pass across the five case files in `docs/cases/`
(Dzaleka, Kakuma–Kalobeyei, Bidibidi, Za'atari, Cox's Bazar). Compiled
2026-10-05 from the case files as refreshed on 2026-09-22. **No new
external research in this pass** — every figure below is carried over
from a case file, which holds the original source, date, and reporting
period. Where a cell says "derived", the number is computed here, not
sourced.

Purpose (per `CONCEPT.md`): find a lever that is simultaneously
**high-leverage**, **tractable**, and **neglected** *relative to the
other cases*. This file does not pick an intervention; it ranks
candidates and lists what must be verified before narrowing.

## 2. Pivot nodes (from each case's causal skeleton)

| Case | Pivot node(s) | What's distinctive |
|---|---|---|
| Dzaleka | **Funding collapse** (single clean pivot) | Funding also stalls the one structural fix (Kayilizi decongestion, US$90M gap) |
| Kakuma | **Funding collapse** | Self-reinforcing loop: ration cuts → unrest (≥5 deaths) → further suspension |
| Bidibidi | **Funding collapse** + **land-allocation scaling failure** | Law is *not* the constraint; implementation is |
| Za'atari | **Exogenous political shock** (Assad's fall) driving both returns *and* donor reprioritization | Risk is a residual, more vulnerable population left as services shrink faster than people |
| Cox's Bazar | **Legal non-recognition** + **funding collapse** (entangled) | Site terrain is itself a hazard; uncontrolled inflow from Myanmar pressures all four pathways |

**Reading:** funding collapse is a pivot in **5/5** cases. That makes it
the most *high-leverage* node — and, by the same token, the least
*neglected* one: it is the problem every actor in the sector is already
talking about. A comparison-driven ToC should look for levers that are
*downstream of or adjacent to* funding collapse but specific enough to be
tractable and under-covered.

needs per-country protection-funding numbers to confirm it's neglected
everywhere and not just at Cox's Bazar. **Do not narrow yet.** Close
G1–G4 first.

## 5. Dzaleka through the other cases

What the comparison adds to Dzaleka specifically:

- **Kakuma → Dzaleka's legal reform:** passing the revised Refugees Act
  won't show up in lived rights by itself. The Dzaleka ToC assumption
  "legal reform → implemented decongestion" needs an explicit monitoring
  step (L3).
- **Bidibidi → Kayilizi:** even under a rights-based framework, land
  allocation fixed at founding-era terms broke after population growth.
  Kayilizi's 330 ha design should be checked against Dzaleka's growth
  rate (~300/month).
- **Kakuma → early warning:** Dzaleka's rations reportedly hit zero in
  mid-2026 and crime rose 70%; Kakuma shows the next step can be
  unrest → further suspension. Worth watching for.
- **Cox's Bazar → protection vacuum endpoint:** where protection funding
  collapses and policing is thin, armed groups fill the gap. Dzaleka's
  documented police-complicity allegations are an early signal of the
  same dynamic.
- **Cox's Bazar → evacuation protocol template:** the only case where a
  formal relocation protocol is known to exist; extracting it gives a
  benchmark for what Dzaleka is missing.
